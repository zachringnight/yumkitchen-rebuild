# live Instagram benchmark

Checked July 14, 2026 against [@yumkitchen](https://www.instagram.com/yumkitchen/). This is a directional creative comparison, not a controlled performance study.

## what the live feed does best

- Real people and recognizable restaurant moments lead the grid: children eating, team members, neighborhood events, guests, and food in use.
- The strongest posts feel immediate and specific rather than campaign-polished.
- Captions are short, lowercase, conversational, and often built around one joke, question, or local detail.
- Food and faces fill most of the frame. Graphic systems support the post instead of replacing the moment.

Visible examples at the time of review:

- [National mac and cheese post](https://www.instagram.com/yumkitchen/p/DaxVVm5Dcxu/): a candid child eating, 20 likes and no comments after roughly six hours.
- [Patticake guessing post](https://www.instagram.com/yumkitchen/p/DanCJNpCHx9/): a child with visible cake evidence, 51 likes and three comments after four days.
- [St. Louis Park fireworks Reel](https://www.instagram.com/yumkitchen/reel/DaVAmcPh3WD/): a timely local moment, 77 likes and one comment after eleven days.

The windows are different, so the counts are not directly comparable. The useful signal is qualitative: people, humor, use, and local immediacy are central to the account's appeal.

## competitor audit cross-check

The June 30 competitive set included captured home-page references from Crumbl, Goldbelly, and SusieCakes. Those captures are commerce and brand references, not social-performance evidence. Use them for hierarchy, appetite, offer clarity, and pacing only.

| Reference | What it does well | Borrow | Avoid | How yum! should beat it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crumbl | Makes the product enormous, gives seasonal moments a simple hook, and creates immediate appetite. | Product filling most of the frame, timely weekly or holiday hooks, very short display copy. | Copying its loud trend language, black type dominance, or novelty-for-novelty's-sake cadence. | Pair equally bold food scale with real guests, team members, meal utility, and four local kitchens. |

| SusieCakes | Feels celebratory and warm, shows an assortment, and keeps cake occasions approachable. | Overhead slice assortments, human celebration cues, warm occasion-led copy. | Borrowing its pastel palette, retro script identity, or generic birthday language. | Own baby blue plus red, real layered Patticake proof, the yum! story, and more specific gifting or table moments. |

### competitor-informed content rules

- At least one product post in every three should let food or cake occupy 75% or more of the frame.
- Build one timely weekly or seasonal hook without changing the durable brand system.
- Show package and product together whenever the post is about gifting or Patticake.
- Use assortment imagery when choice is the story; use one dominant item when appetite is the story.
- Keep yum!'s people and neighborhood presence visible. None of the three references gives yum!'s local restaurant life the same role.
- Test offers through accurate occasions and utility before testing discounts.

### current pack scorecard

- Product scale: strong in lunch, birthday, and Patticake slice assets.
- Packaging memory: strong in the send-cake carousel and gift-drop motion.
- Warm occasion: strong in birthday, event, gifting, and the new human-first lane.
- Conversion clarity: stronger than the live organic feed, with one action per conversion asset.
- Human and local proof: now present, but it should keep growing in future production.
- Remaining gap: a fast-turn seasonal template that can pair one current photo with one timely hook without rebuilding the full campaign.

## where this creative pack should be better

- Keep the live feed's human truth, then add a more consistent baby-blue and red brand memory.
- Give each asset one message and one action. Do not return to three equal copy boxes.
- Use full-bleed photography for the first two-thirds of motion; reserve the baby-blue field for a clear close.
- Keep the real yum! logo visible as a player, not a tiny watermark.
- Use explicit CTAs on conversion posts. Let community and human posts stay lighter and more conversational.
- Use cream, black, and gray only as photographic neutrals. They should not lead the designed system.

## publishing mix

Use the designed assets as punctuation, not the entire feed:

- 50% people, community, kitchen life, guests, and occasions.
- 30% food, bakery, cake, packaging, and visible product use.
- 20% location, catering, gifting, order, and event conversion creative.

In every nine-post run, include at least three human moments, two food-first posts, one community or local moment, one Patticake occasion, and no more than two designed conversion posts.

## caption pattern

1. One concrete moment or food detail.
2. One human, kitchen, location, or occasion detail.
3. One action only when the post needs it.

Examples:

- The people behind your yum! Real kitchens, a real team, and food made from scratch.
- Cake evidence everywhere. The only question is who gets the next slice.
- Lunch meeting? Tell us the date and headcount. We will take it from there.
- Four kitchens. Pick the one closest to the craving.

## rights gate

The live account is a creative reference, not an automatic asset library. Customer, child, creator, or review imagery needs source and usage-right confirmation before promotional or paid reuse. The production human-first asset in this pack uses current high-resolution photography already published by the owned yum! website.
